=== CHECK ENGINE LIGHT ===
The check engine light (CEL) can mean many things from a loose fuel cap to serious engine issues.
Common causes: loose or missing fuel cap, faulty oxygen sensor, catalytic converter problem,
mass airflow sensor failure, spark plug or ignition coil issues.
If the light is solid (not flashing), you can usually drive safely to a mechanic soon.
If it is FLASHING, stop driving immediately — a flashing CEL means active misfiring that can
damage the catalytic converter. First check: tighten the fuel cap and drive for a day or two.
 
=== BATTERY PROBLEMS ===
A dead or weak battery is one of the most common car problems.
Signs: car won't start, slow cranking sound, clicking when you turn the key, battery warning light.
Average battery life is 3 to 5 years. If your car won't start, try jump-starting it.
If it starts after a jump but dies again soon, the battery likely needs replacement.
Check for corroded terminals (white or blue powder) — clean with baking soda and water.
A mechanic or auto parts store can test battery health for free.
 
=== OVERHEATING ===
If the temperature gauge rises into the red zone, your car is overheating.
Immediately turn off the air conditioning and turn on the heater to transfer heat away from the engine.
If the temperature keeps rising, safely pull over and switch off the engine.
DO NOT open the radiator cap when the engine is hot — it is pressurised and can cause severe burns.
Common causes: low coolant level, broken thermostat, failed water pump, leaking radiator.
Check coolant level only when the engine is completely cold.
 
=== TYRE PROBLEMS ===
Common tyre issues: flat tyre, low pressure (TPMS light on), uneven wear, bulge on sidewall.
Recommended tyre pressure: check the sticker inside your driver's door — usually 32 to 35 PSI.
A bulge in the sidewall means the tyre is structurally damaged — replace it immediately, do not drive on it.
Uneven wear usually indicates wheel alignment or suspension problems.
Rotate tyres every 5,000 to 7,500 miles to ensure even wear.
 
=== STRANGE NOISES ===
Squealing when braking: worn brake pads — replace soon.
Grinding when braking: brake pads completely worn, metal on metal — stop driving if possible.
Knocking or ticking from engine: low oil level, worn engine bearings, or carbon buildup.
Clicking when turning: worn CV (constant velocity) joint — common in front-wheel drive cars.
Rattling from underneath: loose heat shield, exhaust pipe, or suspension component.
Humming from wheels: wheel bearing failure — worsens at higher speeds and during turns.
 
=== BRAKE PROBLEMS ===
Spongy or soft brake pedal: air in brake lines or brake fluid leak — DO NOT DRIVE.
Car pulls to one side when braking: uneven brake pad wear or a stuck calliper.
Grinding noise when braking: worn brake pads or rotors — replace immediately.
Brake warning light on: low brake fluid or handbrake not fully released.
Brakes are a safety-critical system. Any brake issue should be inspected by a mechanic immediately.
 
=== AIR CONDITIONING ===
AC not cooling: low refrigerant (most common cause), failed compressor, or clogged cabin filter.
Musty smell from vents: mould or bacteria in the evaporator — use an AC cleaner spray.
Weak airflow: clogged cabin air filter — replace every 12,000 to 15,000 miles (easy DIY fix).
Refrigerant recharge is an inexpensive service most garages can do quickly.
 
=== FLUID LEAKS ===
Brown or black oily puddle under engine: engine oil leak — check oil level immediately.
Green, orange, or pink puddle: coolant leak — top up and see a mechanic soon.
Clear water under car near front passenger side in summer: normal AC condensation, not a leak.
Dark brown fluid near wheels: brake fluid leak — STOP DRIVING immediately, safety critical.
Red fluid leak: transmission fluid or power steering fluid — see mechanic soon.
 
=== FUEL ECONOMY ===
Sudden drop in fuel efficiency: dirty air filter, underinflated tyres, faulty oxygen sensor,
worn spark plugs, or a dragging brake. Check tyre pressure first (free and easy).
Replace air filter if needed (cheap DIY). If the problem persists, get a diagnostic scan.
 
=== GENERAL MAINTENANCE ===
Oil change: every 5,000 to 7,500 km for conventional oil, every 10,000 to 15,000 km for synthetic.
Tyre rotation: every 8,000 to 12,000 km. Tyre replacement: when tread depth is below 2/32 inch.
Brake pads: every 40,000 to 100,000 km depending on driving style.
Air filter: every 20,000 to 30,000 km. Spark plugs: every 30,000 to 100,000 km.
Coolant flush: every 2 years or 40,000 km. Battery: test every 2 years, replace after 5 years.
 
=== VIBRATION AND HANDLING ===
Steering wheel vibrates at high speed: unbalanced tyres or wheels out of alignment.
Car pulls to one side: wheel alignment problem or uneven tyre pressure.
Vibration when braking: warped brake rotors — the disc-shaped parts the pads press against.
Shaking when accelerating: worn CV joints or unbalanced driveshaft.
All of these can be diagnosed quickly at any tyre or alignment shop.
 
=== LIGHTS AND WIPERS ===
Headlight flickering: loose connection, failing alternator, or dying bulb.
One headlight out: most likely a blown bulb — inexpensive to replace yourself.
Wiper streaks: worn rubber blade — replace every 6 to 12 months, very inexpensive.
Cracked windscreen: small chips can be repaired cheaply. Large cracks require full replacement.
ABS warning light: anti-lock braking system fault — normal brakes still work but ABS is disabled.
